Herrera Ranch | @29.283056,-98.614028 | This Von Ormy ranch, with its jacales, belonged to Blas Herrera, the Tejano scout who rode into San Antonio in mid-February 1836 to warn residents of the approaching Mexican army; according to its National Register nomination, it first appeared on the tax rolls in his name in 1845 — 800 acres valued at $300, and three cows. | https://abc7.com/archive/8331927/
Old Alamo National Bank Building | @29.423833,-98.489167 | Built in 1902 as the headquarters for Alamo National Bank, this 8,000-ton building was lifted off its foundation with 1,800 screw jacks in the early 1910s and shifted back on its site on steel rollers to escape demolition as the city of San Antonio widened east Commerce Street. | https://sanantonioreport.org/?p=5351861 | new
Alamo National Bank Building | @29.424167,-98.491667 | Conceived just before the stock market crash of 1929 and built in 1929-30, this 24-story bank tower by Graham, Anderson, Probst and White — one of the country's finest architects of tall buildings — rises from a 3-story granite base on a site bounded by three major thoroughfares and the San Antonio River. | https://atlas.thc.state.tx.us/NR/pdfs/06000364/06000364.pdf
Altgelt-Isbell House | @29.415556,-98.491944 | Ernst Altgelt, who had helped establish the Hill Country town of Comfort, commissioned this limestone house in 1876 but died in 1878 just before it was completed; his earlier house just to the south is said to have been the street's first, which gave him the honor of naming it for Kaiser Wilhelm I of Prussia, and the two-story side gallery with its outside stairway is the only one of its kind left in the neighborhood. | https://www.sahouseregistry.com/houses/226-king-william
Fourth Ward School | @29.417222,-98.486667 | The City of San Antonio constructed this building in 1875 with a fire station on the first floor and the Fourth Ward School on the second; the entire structure became the Seventh Ward School in 1882, and it later served as the San Antonio Independent School District Administration Office. | https://atlas.thc.texas.gov/NR/pdfs/78002891/78002891.pdf | new
Gilbeau Slave Quarters | @29.419444,-98.495 | The San Antonio Express reported on July 6, 1952, that these Guilbeau slave quarters had been saved while the Guilbeau house was set for razing; later that summer the house went, while the Conservation Society worked to save six others. | https://farmandranch.omeka.net/exhibits/show/guilbeauhouse/gilbeaunewspaperarticles
Louis Gresser House | @29.4212,-98.4898 | Louis Gresser paid $1,000 in 1861 for this corner property at Presa and Nueva Streets, which came with a small house, and the Gressers built a house adjoining it; legend says the famous Texas Ranger Colonel John Coffee "Jack" Hays once lived on the property, and the Conservation Society of San Antonio, which bought it in 1976, uses it as the office for A Night in Old San Antonio. | https://saconservation.org/?p=4301 | new
James Butler Bonham Elementary School | @29.413889,-98.488889 | Founded in 1889 as the Garden Street School, this is one of the oldest schools in the San Antonio Independent School District; it was renamed in 1902 for James Butler Bonham, who died at the Battle of the Alamo. | https://schools.saisd.net/page/107.about
Jose Antonio Navarro Elementary School | @29.4197,-98.5022 | This Italianate school, named for Jose Antonio Navarro, grew in stages — its National Register listing marks 1885, 1902 and 1923 — to designs by the firms Phelps & Shand and Klauss & Witte. | https://npgallery.nps.gov/AssetDetail/8c86b61f-23c1-42f4-aae4-fcea6e161fdd
